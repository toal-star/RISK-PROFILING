Benefits Trafficking SNAP
Problem.
SNAP benefits trafficking — where authorized retailers exchange food assistance for cash, stealing from low-income families and defrauding the federal government — goes largely undetected because no accessible tool exists to automatically surface suspicious retailers from publicly available data.
Solution.
Benefits Trafficking SNAP is an AI-powered risk profiling tool that ingests public USDA retailer and disqualification data, scores authorized stores by their likelihood of trafficking activity, and delivers plain-English explanations of why each flagged retailer looks suspicious.
Target User.
Nonprofit food security investigators, state-level social services agencies, academic researchers, and investigative journalists who lack access to closed federal fraud detection systems but need data-driven reasonable suspicion to prioritize their limited investigative resources.
Success Criteria.

Reduces the time to generate a retailer risk profile from hours of manual spreadsheet work to a few minutes 
Correctly flags a statistically significant percentage of retailers already on the USDA's published disqualification list when run as a validation test
Produces risk explanations clear enough that a non-technical user can understand and act on them without additional training
To reduce lump data inputs, prioritize specific regions, or a specific region, effectively, for the purpose of the contest 
Demonstrates measurable pattern differences between flagged and non-flagged retailers across store type, location, and authorization history
